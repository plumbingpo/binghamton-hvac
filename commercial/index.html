Kitchen Climate Control

Comprehensive Food-Service HVAC Systems

Restaurants and food-service businesses have climate demands that ordinary equipment can't meet. Kitchens generate enormous heat loads while dining rooms need to stay comfortable, exhaust hoods pull out conditioned air that makeup air units must replace, and a busy Friday night pushes rooftop units to their limit. Binghamton HVAC specializes in food-service HVAC, including rooftop package units, split systems, proper tonnage and airflow sizing, makeup air balancing against hood exhaust, and scheduled service that works around your hours.

Air balance is where many restaurant HVAC problems start. When a kitchen runs negative, it pulls dining-room air in the wrong direction, sucks hot or cold outdoor air through every door, and wears out your staff at peak service. We balance the makeup air to the hood exhaust and log every visit in written reports that landlords and auditors can review.

Equipment replacements and maintenance schedules are planned with owners, managers, and kitchen leads, so everything fits your operating schedule. When a job can't happen during service, we install during off-hours, keeping the kitchen running and the dining room comfortable in the meantime.

Safe Installation

Installation, Inspection, and Record-Keeping

Every commercial job we take on starts with safety. Our technicians inspect heat exchangers for cracks, confirm correct venting and combustion air, check electrical connections and disconnects, document the refrigerant charge, protect against condensate overflow, and check ventilation rates in occupied areas. The goal is straightforward: no carbon monoxide hazards, no poor air quality, no refrigerant leaks, and no ceiling tiles ruined by an overflowing drain. You receive a written report that ranks each issue by priority and recommends a specific fix.

The paperwork matters as much as the repair. Every account gets a documentation binder with detailed service logs and temperature and filter records, giving you proof of due diligence for landlords, insurers, and auditors. Equipment that works hardest, in kitchens, gyms, and large mechanical rooms, gets a more frequent service schedule.
