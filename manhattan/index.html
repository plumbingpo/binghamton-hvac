When Your System Fails in Manhattan

Here's what we see most often when something breaks at home: an air conditioner blowing warm air, a condenser that hums but won't start, a furnace that lights for a few seconds and quits, a breaker that trips every time the system kicks on, an evaporator coil that has frozen, a condensate drain backed up into the furnace cabinet, or a thermostat that has quit. A little caution while you wait for us can head off a much bigger repair. An iced-over AC should be shut off at the thermostat with the fan set to "on", which lets the coil thaw faster. Running it frozen can flood the drain pan and damage the compressor. A short-cycling furnace, or any burning smell, means it's time to shut the unit off at its service switch and call us immediately.

Keeping Your Manhattan HVAC System Running Longer

The best way to avoid a mid-season breakdown is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners replace filters every one to three months, keep shrubs and grass trimmed two feet back from the outdoor condenser, rinse the condenser coil each spring, and have a professional tune-up twice a year, cooling in spring and heating in fall, so small problems get caught while they're still inexpensive.

How old is your system? Its age says a lot about what comes next. Most well-maintained air conditioners reach 12 to 15 years, gas furnaces 15 to 20, and heat pumps 10 to 15. If your system is beyond that range and also shows higher bills, rooms that never quite reach temperature, or frequent repairs, it's a strong candidate for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than an emergency replacement in peak season.

If comfort problems do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A weak capacitor is a quick fix. The same capacitor ignored for a season can take the compressor with it and turn a minor repair into a full system replacement. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and sleepless nights.

Typical Heating & Cooling Issues in Manhattan

Most local HVAC failures announce themselves before they happen. A breaker that trips more than once, a condenser that hums louder each month, rooms drifting further from the thermostat setting, energy bills creeping up with nothing else changed. Those are early signs of failing capacitors, refrigerant leaks, dirty coils, and duct problems, and caught early they're an affordable repair. Ignored, they take out compressors and heat exchangers, the two most expensive parts in the system. We diagnose the cause, not just the symptom, so small problems stay small.
